<objective>
Turn an attachment into a stream of pixels, at a cost the loop can afford.

Purpose: this is the plan the readback spike existed for, and the one where the wrong obvious answer
is very close to hand. `capture_now` already paints a webview and reads it back, and
`encode_screenshot` already turns the result into a PNG. Building the frame path on them would
compile, would look right on a simple page, and would be wrong three separate ways: the screenshot
encoder measured **21 ms on a page-like frame and 175 ms on a photo-like one** against a 30 ms budget
before a byte leaves the machine; the capture queue waits for a frame-ready notification with a
one-and-a-half-second deadline, which at thirty-three ticks a second is thirty-three dead deadlines
and a static page that delivers nothing until each one expires; and the show-and-hide cycle it
performs per call is a different cost profile from holding a tab shown once.

The right answers are all measured. The engine's own documentation says an embedder may paint and
present without a prior frame-ready notification, which licenses painting unconditionally on the tick
and letting the tile comparison decide whether anything is worth sending. The same PNG crate at a
fast compression setting with a subtractive filter over 64×64 tile diffs measured microseconds for
interactive changes and about two milliseconds for a full keyframe, with a whole-frame comparison at
about a seventh of a millisecond — all of which 05-02 re-measured against real engine output rather
than synthetic frames. And the readback buffer is sendable across threads, so the comparison and the
encode leave the loop entirely.

`encode_screenshot` stays exactly as it is. A one-shot agent screenshot should be small and lossless
and a frame stream should be fast and incremental; sharing an encoder between them would make
screenshots enormous or frames slow. The frame encoder copies its **structure** and diverges on three
lines.

Output: a visibility hold that survives an unrelated tab-set change, a tick joined into the loop's
own wait computation, an encoder thread, and the end-to-end assertions that a keyframe arrives, that
deltas follow, and that a static page is silent.
</objective>

**Read `05-02-SPIKE.md` first, and read its instruction section specifically.** It carries the
measured readback distribution under the harness's software rendering and, where the machine allowed
it, on real hardware; the measured tile-comparison and encode costs against real engine output; the
dirty-tile count that should trigger a keyframe; the fastest rung the ladder may claim; and — the part
this plan is written against — **what to do if the software-rendered figure cannot sustain the
cadence.** Follow that instruction rather than improvising one. If it says the pump requests the
cadence regardless and the latency suite asserts the requested cadence at a rung the harness can
sustain, do that; if the numbers pointed somewhere else, the spike says where.

**The capture sequence to copy, and the two parts not to** (`app.rs:399-421`). Copy: paint, take the
context's size as signed integers, build a rectangle from the origin and that size, read it to an
image, and treat a failed read as a value rather than a panic. Do **not** copy the re-hide, because
the hold is for the life of the attachment; do **not** copy the call into the screenshot encoder,
because the pump hands the buffer to a channel instead.

**The encoder to sit beside without disturbing** (`app.rs:2494-2519`). It builds an encoder, sets
colour and depth, writes a header with a fallible-step-else-return on each stage, writes the image
data, and base64-encodes the result into a payload. `05-PATTERNS.md` is explicit about what the frame
encoder does with it: copy the **structure**, and diverge on exactly three lines — set a fast
compression level, set a subtractive filter, and drop the base64 entirely because the frame channel
is binary. The existing function's one call site is the capture helper, and that count must not change.

**The visibility interaction that will bite if it is not handled.** `sync_visibility` runs on every
tab-set change and hides every tab that is not the displayed one, blurring it as it goes. A tab held
shown for a viewer would be hidden the next time the local human opened a tab, switched view, or
closed one — and the viewer's frames would stop with nothing erroring. Give the tab table a
held-for-viewing marker the synchronisation consults before hiding. Two details matter: a held tab is
**shown but not focused** (focus belongs to the tab the local human is driving), and the marker is a
count rather than a flag, because two viewers may hold the same tab and the first to detach must not
release it.

**Why holding a background tab shown is safe, and why to assert it anyway.** Every tab owns its own
offscreen rendering context (`tabs.rs:47`), and the capture helper's own doc comment states the
consequence: captures cause no visible flicker because the displayed tab's framebuffer is its own.
05-02 was asked to confirm it by observation rather than by citation. Assert it here too, end to end:
the local display is unchanged while a viewer is attached to a different tab.

**The tick joins the existing wait computation.** `next_capture_deadline` feeds `set_wait`
(`app.rs:678-680`, `:1217`). The pump's next deadline joins that computation. Do not install a second
control-flow source: two things deciding when the loop wakes is how a loop stops waking.

**The deferred-queue idiom, and why it is load-bearing here** (`app.rs:423-451`). The scoped borrow
that is dropped before the work runs is the part that matters — the shared state's cells are borrowed
from engine callbacks too, and callbacks use the try-and-skip form rather than panicking
(`app.rs:2816-2827`). A pump that held a borrow across a readback would be a borrow held across the
slowest thing in the loop.

**The encoder thread's spawn shape** (`http.rs:870-893`, not `control.rs:85-95`). Named thread, a
match on the runtime or channel construction with a report on failure, and a check on the spawn
itself rather than an assertion. A browser whose frame encoder could not start is still a browser;
the viewer gets a state and the local human gets nothing worse than a log line.

**What the frame header carries**, from 05-03: the version, the tab, the frame sequence, the kind,
the tile origin and size, the full frame size, the scale denominator, and the last input sequence the
server had applied when it painted. 05-06 records that last value on the connection; this plan reads
it at paint time and stamps it. It is the whole of the latency instrumentation and the reason a client
can discard a frame that predates its own most recent input.

**What the pump must never do**, from the anti-pattern list: wait for a frame-ready notification;
comparison or encode on the winit thread; use the capture queue; hide the tab between frames; or
change what the local human is looking at. A viewer attaching must not pull the human's window onto
another tab, and the human switching tabs must not redirect or interrupt a viewer.
</interfaces>

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| the frame pump → the winit main thread | Every millisecond the pump spends is a millisecond the local browser is not painting; this is the boundary where a remote feature can make the local product unusable |
| a viewer's attachment → the local human's window | An attachment holds a tab shown; if that reached the visible tab, the view mode or focus, a remote peer would be steering the human's own screen |
| a rendered agent page → a viewer's screen | Pixels of a page one agent is driving, delivered to a viewer authorised as a possibly different client |
| the encoder thread's retained buffers → process memory | One full-surface buffer per live attachment is the bound; without an explicit release it is a leak per reconnect |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-05-12 | Denial of Service | the frame pump starving the winit loop, making the **local** browser unusable while a viewer is attached | medium | mitigate | The comparison and the encode leave the main thread entirely — the readback buffer is sendable, and that is the whole design — so the loop spends only the readback, whose cost 05-02 measured as a distribution and expressed as a fraction of the local frame time. The cadence joins the loop's existing wait computation rather than installing a second control-flow source, borrows are dropped before the readback, a static page costs one comparison and sends nothing, and the concurrent-attachment cap 05-05 set bounds how many ticks can be due at once |
| T-05-12-A | Denial of Service | the encoder thread retaining a full-surface buffer per attachment forever | medium | mitigate | The previous-frame buffer is per attachment and released when the attachment ends, with the bound stated in a comment: one buffer per live attachment, capped by the attachment cap. Unit-tested through the attachment lifecycle rather than argued |
| T-05-12-B | Denial of Service | an encoder thread that cannot start, stalling viewers silently | low | mitigate | The degrade-and-report spawn shape: a match on construction with an event reported, and a checked spawn rather than an assertion. Viewers get a named state; the local browser is unaffected, which is the same posture the listener already takes |
| T-05-04-E | Elevation of Privilege | an attachment steering what the local human sees — pulling the window onto another tab, changing the view mode, or stealing focus | high | mitigate | A held tab is shown and **not** focused, the hold is a count consulted by the visibility synchronisation rather than a call into it, and the view module touches no active-tab setter, no view mode and no focus — asserted by a source criterion returning 0 and by an end-to-end read of both values over a different channel before and after a full frame exchange |
| T-05-10 | Information Disclosure | a viewer receiving pixels of a page a different agent is driving | medium | accept | The same deliberate decision 05-05 recorded and for the same reason: the human is the trust root and a remote human is the trust root at a distance, so a viewer sees what the local Agents view shows. Bounded by who holds a token. Recorded here again because this is the plan where it stops being a tab list and becomes page content, which is a materially larger disclosure and deserves the second look |
| T-05-13-A | Information Disclosure | page pixels retained in memory after a viewer is gone | low | mitigate | The per-attachment buffer is released on detach, disconnect and tab close. Nothing is written to disk: this phase persists no frame, no manifest and no page content, which is a deliberate difference from Phase 5.1's manifest work |
| T-05-11-C | Denial of Service | a malformed header assembled from a degenerate frame — a zero-sized tile, or a region exceeding the surface | medium | mitigate | The wire module refuses both at decode (05-03); this plan's assembly is unit-tested against the same edges — a single-pixel change at the right edge, the bottom edge and the corner, where partial tiles live — because an off-by-one there produces a stripe of stale pixels that reads as a rendering bug rather than as a protocol error |
| T-05-SC | Tampering | package installs | n/a | accept | No dependency change; the PNG crate was already a direct dependency and 05-01 landed the phase's one addition with its audit recorded there |

ASVS level 1; block on high. The one high-severity item is mitigated **within this plan** and
mitigated structurally: an attachment cannot steer the local window because the view module holds no
reference to anything that could, asserted by a source criterion returning zero and by an end-to-end
read over a channel other than the one under test.

**Stated rather than mitigated:** the readback is unconditional, because the engine supplies no damage
rectangle — its frame-ready notification carries only the webview. That is why the readback cost is
the phase's measured unknown rather than an optimisable one, and why 05-02 exists.
