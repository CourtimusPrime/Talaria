<objective>
Make the latency target a deliverable rather than a hope — and make the case where it cannot be met a
behaviour rather than a disappointment.

Purpose: Success Criterion 2 names roughly thirty to sixty milliseconds during takeover on a **direct**
path and two hundred to five hundred passive. `05-RESEARCH.md` measured what that costs and found the
budget achievable on a direct WireGuard path and unachievable on a relayed one, on this developer's own
hardware: a direct path on the local network measured 342–447 Mbit/s and the same machine tethered
through a phone fell to a relayed path at about 13 Mbit/s, where a single full keyframe takes roughly
three hundred and twenty milliseconds to transmit before any latency is added. No codec available to
this project changes that.

So D-05-06 decided the product behaviour: **degrade and report; never refuse.** A criterion that
silently fails on a relayed link is worse than one that reports, and refusing takeover would fail the
core value in exactly the situation it exists for — a degraded takeover of a login wall still clears
the login wall.

This plan also answers the two sharpest questions this phase's requirements raise, because they are
numeric rather than abstract. **What exactly triggers the passive-to-takeover transition, and what
happens at sixty milliseconds and at sixty-one?** Those are design questions with defensible answers,
and answers that are not written down become answers somebody discovers by testing. Both are stated
here, both are unit-tested at the boundary and one unit either side, and the ladder's rungs live in one
ordered table both ends read — the same discipline the existing timeout ordering follows, which
`05-PATTERNS.md` identified as the only thing in this codebase that resembles a ladder.

Output: a rung table in the shared vocabulary, a server that honours a rung, a client controller with
a smoothed estimate and hysteresis, honest reporting copy, an unprivileged link shim, and the suite
that proves the transition from frame timestamps and the degradation under a reproduction of this
tailnet's own measured relayed profile.
</objective>

<interfaces>
**The ladder, and where its numbers come from.** `05-RESEARCH.md` proposes five rungs: full resolution
at the driven interval, full resolution at twice it, half resolution at twice it, half resolution at
four times it, and passive only. Take that shape, and take the **numbers** from `05-02-SPIKE.md`,
which measured the readback and encode on real engine output and stated the fastest rung the ladder
may claim. A ladder whose top rung is faster than the server can actually produce is a ladder that
starts one step down and never notices.

The passive interval must sit inside the two-hundred-to-five-hundred-millisecond band DIST-02 names.
The research measured a half-resolution frame at about a seventh of a millisecond to produce and about
a quarter of the bytes, so the passive rung's cost is not the constraint; pick a value in the band and
say why.

**Where the rungs live.** In the shared vocabulary, as an ordered table, with each entry carrying its
interval in whole milliseconds and its scale denominator. Both ends read that table. `05-PATTERNS.md`
notes there is no analog in this codebase for a controller with hysteresis, and names the one property
worth copying from the nearest thing in spirit — the timeout ordering, where every bound is named in
one place and the ordering between them is load-bearing: **name every rung in one place, and make the
ordering a unit-tested invariant.** Do that literally: a test that walks the table and asserts the
intervals are non-decreasing and the denominators non-decreasing, so a later edit that inserted a rung
out of order fails rather than producing a ladder that steps sideways.

**The cadence message changes shape, deliberately.** 05-03 shipped a cadence request carrying a raw
interval. Replace it with one naming a **rung**. The reason is worth the churn: an interval a client
invents is an interval the server has to decide whether to honour, which means a validation rule, a
clamp and a disagreement about what happens at the edges — whereas a rung is an enumerated value with
one fixed meaning at both ends. Say so in the message's doc comment. The server refuses an
unrecognised rung the way it refuses everything else: with no reason.

**The measurement, and why it needs no synchronised clock.** The client records the send time against
each input sequence it emits. Every frame header echoes the last input sequence the server had applied
when it painted. When a frame arrives echoing sequence *n*, the elapsed time since *n* was sent is an
input-to-photon sample across two machines with no clock agreement required. That is the entire reason
the frame header carries the field, and it is why the sample is honest in a way a round-trip ping
would not be — it includes the readback, the encode, the transmission and the client's own
presentation.

Feed samples into an exponentially weighted moving average. That average is the **only** floating-point
value in this controller. Everything else — intervals, thresholds, counts — is whole milliseconds or
whole counts.

**The boundary rules, stated because they will be tested.**

*Passive to driven, and back.* An attachment enters the driven cadence on the **first accepted input**
for that tab on that connection. It returns to the passive cadence once the elapsed time since the last
accepted input **reaches** the idle threshold. An input arriving exactly at the threshold re-enters the
driven cadence, because a new input always does. One comparison on elapsed-since-last, no countdown,
and therefore no race between two clocks. 05-08 established this on the server; this plan is where the
client's controller agrees with it, and the two must be the same rule, not two rules that happen to
match.

*Overrun.* A sample **strictly greater** than the current rung's interval is an overrun; a sample
exactly equal to it is not. So at a sixty-millisecond rung, sixty is fine and sixty-one is an overrun.
Arbitrary until somebody tests it; stated, it is a contract.

*Movement.* Step **down** one rung after a small number of consecutive overruns. Step **up** one rung
after a larger number of consecutive samples comfortably under the interval — under a margin fraction
of it, not merely under it. Two asymmetries, both deliberate: the different counts mean a brief spike
degrades slowly and a sustained improvement recovers slowly, and the margin means a link sitting
exactly at a rung's edge does not oscillate between two rungs forever. Name both counts and the margin
as constants with a doc comment saying which asymmetry each provides.

**The reduction, and its rounding.** A rung with a denominator above one produces frames at reduced
dimensions. Round the reduced dimensions **up**, so a surface whose width is not divisible by the
denominator loses no column — a floor would drop the rightmost strip, which reads as a rendering
fault. The header already carries the source dimensions and the denominator, so the client reconstructs
the placement exactly rather than inferring a scale from a size ratio, which is why 05-09 was told to
honour the field before anything set it above one.

**The report, and its register.** `04-UI-SPEC.md` is the register: the fact first, the next step
second, no exclamation, no reassurance. The research is specific about the content — not a bit rate,
but what the human cares about, naming the current rung and, where the platform can say so, **why**.
The overlay network reports whether a peer's path is direct or relayed, and reading it is the
difference between "the browser is slow" and "your connection is relayed" — the same diagnosis this
developer's own notes record having had to make by hand for an unrelated service. Read it if it is
available and degrade the copy gracefully if it is not: a report that requires a subprocess to succeed
is a report that disappears when it matters.

**The shim.** `05-RESEARCH.md` § Validation Architecture specifies it: a standard-library forwarder
between the client and the server with three knobs — an added one-way delay, a token-bucket rate cap,
and a kill that closes both halves — in roughly eighty lines. It needs no privileges, which the kernel
traffic-shaping alternatives both do, and the continuous-integration machine is a single unprivileged
host. Give it this tailnet's own measured relayed profile as a named default: about 13 Mbit/s and about
forty milliseconds. The kill knob is not used by this plan and is included anyway, because Phase 5.1's
reconnect work needs exactly it and writing it twice is how two versions disagree.

**What this suite must not claim.** `05-RESEARCH.md` Pitfall 2 names it: measuring on loopback and
declaring the budget met. Loopback hides transmission entirely, which is the only variable that
matters. This suite asserts the **cadence transition**, the **ladder's response** under the shim, and
the **reporting** — all of which are real properties of the product. The target itself is a
direct-path claim and belongs to the manual two-machine check `05-VALIDATION.md` already lists, which
05-11 turns into a script.
</interfaces>

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| a client's cadence request → the server's tick rate | A request the server honours without bound is a way for one viewer to make the engine's loop spend everything it has |
| the controller → the human's ability to drive | A controller with any path that suppresses input would turn a slow link into a lost session |
| a measurement on loopback → a claim about the product | A timing assertion that hides transmission would let the criterion be marked met on evidence that does not bear on it |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-05-12-D | Denial of Service | a client requesting an arbitrary interval and pinning the engine's loop at it | high | mitigate | A cadence request names an **enumerated rung**, not a number: the fastest rung is fixed in a table the server reads, and it is bounded below by the production cost 05-02 measured. An unrecognised rung is refused with no reason. This is why the message shape changed — an interval a client invents is an interval the server must decide whether to honour, and every such decision is a clamp somebody can get wrong |
| T-05-12-E | Denial of Service | the ladder oscillating between two rungs on a link at a rung's edge, producing continuous keyframes | medium | mitigate | Asymmetric counts and a recovery margin, both named as constants with the asymmetry each provides, and a unit test that drives a long run of samples sitting exactly at the interval and asserts the rung does not move. A rung change forces a keyframe, which is precisely why oscillation is expensive rather than merely untidy |
| T-05-06-A | Repudiation | the product silently missing its stated latency target on a relayed link | high | mitigate | D-05-06's behaviour, implemented and asserted: the client walks down the ladder and **reports**, naming the rung and — where the platform can be asked — whether the path is direct or relayed. The end-to-end suite reproduces this tailnet's own measured relayed profile through an unprivileged shim and asserts both the step-down and the report. A criterion that quietly fails is worse than one that says so |
| T-05-06-B | Denial of Service | a slow link being treated as a reason to refuse takeover | high | mitigate | The controller has **no** refusal path — asserted by a source criterion returning 0 across the rejection spellings — and the constrained-link suite asserts a click still reached the page. D-05-06 is explicit: a degraded takeover of a login wall still clears the login wall, which is the entire product, so refusing would fail the core value in the exact situation it exists for |
| T-05-06-C | Repudiation | a loopback measurement standing in for a direct-path claim | medium | mitigate | The suite's docstring states what it will not claim, every timing assertion carries its tolerance and the reasoning for it, and the target itself stays a manual two-machine row rather than migrating into an automated assertion that cannot bear it. This is the pitfall the research names by name and its warning sign is a latency assertion running against loopback |
| T-05-11-E | Denial of Service | a reduced-resolution frame with degenerate dimensions | medium | mitigate | The reduction rounds up, so a source dimension not divisible by the denominator yields a destination that covers every pixel rather than dropping the rightmost or bottom strip; the wire type already refuses a zero-sized tile and a tile exceeding its declared frame; and the divisibility case is unit-tested with a deliberately awkward width |
| T-05-SC | Tampering | package installs | n/a | accept | No dependency change; the shim is standard library only, which is also why it works unprivileged on a single-slot continuous-integration machine where the kernel traffic-shaping alternatives do not |

ASVS level 1; block on high. The three high-severity items are mitigated **within this plan**: the
unbounded-cadence case by making the request an enumerated rung rather than a number, which removes the
decision rather than bounding it; the silent-miss case by implementing and asserting the report; and the
refuse case by having no refusal path at all and proving a click still lands under the constrained link.
